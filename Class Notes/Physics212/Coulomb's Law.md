# Coulomb's Law

[[Class Notes/Physics212/Physics212|← Physics212]]

> [!equation] [[Equations/Coulomb's Law|Coulomb's Law]]
> <a id="eq:CoulombsLaw"></a>
> Gives a force along the line of two charges. If $q_1$ and $q_2$ have the same sign (i.e. charge) then the force must be repulsive. But if they have the *same* sign, then it must be attractive. Because of Newton's second law, $F_{12} = -F_{21}$
>

$$
\vec{F_{12}} =k\frac{q_1q_2}{r_{12}^2}\hat{r_{12}}
$$

  
  

![[physics212-coulombs-law-1.png]]

    

![[physics212-coulombs-law-2.png]]

  

$$
\boxed{F_{1,net} = F_{21} + F_{31}}
$$

  

![[physics212-coulombs-law-3.png]]

> [!equation] [[Equations/Superposition Principle|Superposition Principle]]
> <a id="eq:SuperpostionPrinciple"></a>
>

$$
F_{net} = \sum_i \vec{F_i}
$$

> [!constant] Electron Charge
> <a id="const:e"></a>
>

$$
e = -1.6 \times 10^{-19} C
$$

> [!constant] k
> <a id="const:k"></a>
>

$$
k = 9 \times 10^9 \frac{NM^2}{C^2}
$$

### Examples

---

> [!example] Example
>
> $\boxed{F_{3,net}= ???}$

>
>
> ![[physics212-coulombs-law-4.png]]
>
> ---
>
> Using Coulomb's Law [(eq:CoulombsLaw)](#eq:CoulombsLaw)…
>
> $$
>
> \vec{F_{3,net}} = \vec{F_{31}} + \vec{F_{32}} 
> $$
>
> $$
> \vec{F_{31}} = k \frac{q_3q_1}{r_{31}^2} \hat{r_{32}}
> $$
>
> $$
> \vec{F_{32}} = k \frac{q_3q_2}{r_{32}^2} \hat{r_{32}}
> $$
>
> $$
> k = 9 \times 10^9 \frac{NM^2}{C^2},     q_1 = 2\mu C,    q_2 = -1 \mu C,    q_3 = 4\mu C 
> $$
>
> $$
> \vec{F_{31}} = 1.800\times10^{-02}\,\mathrm{N},   \vec{F_{32}} = -3.600\times10^{-02}\,\mathrm{N}
> $$
>
> $$
> $\boxed{\vec{F}_{3, net} =  -1.800\times10^{-02}\,\mathrm{N}}$

> $$
