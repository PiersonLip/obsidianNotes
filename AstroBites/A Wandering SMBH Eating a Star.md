# A Wandering Supermassive Black Hole Eating a Star [@Saad2026]

[Astrobites post](https://astrobites.org/2026/03/02/a-wandering-supermassive-black-hole-eating-a-star/)

---

- Covers a paper investigating a [[TDE]]s discovered *away* from the center of a galaxy, denoted as AT2024tvd.
- BH mass `determines accretion disk behavior`, with more massive BHs having larger, cooler disks
- This can be measured with the disks' brightness
- This was observed with [[Pan-STARRS]], [[Swift Observatory]], [[Pan-STARRS]], and [[XMM-Newton]]
- [[TDE]] start with an initial burst in visible and UV which is not very well understood, but after a period of time (few hundred days) the visible and UV light start to come from the accretion disk
- Used a model called kerrSED, which measures spectral energy distribution (SED), this accounts for disk temp, physical size, spin, viewing angle, and [[Comptonization]]
- From this fitting they found that the BH had a mass of $∼ M_\odot$, classifying it as a [[SMBH]]
- This puts it in a unique spot, as previously detected [[TDE]]s were located in Ultra-Compact Dwarf Galaxies, small dense collections of stars which orbit larger host galaxies.
- AT2024tvd has nearly no surrounding stars, suggesting that it is `wandering', having being ejected form a galaxy merger and now slowly drifting towards the galactic center, sheding stars as it goes.
