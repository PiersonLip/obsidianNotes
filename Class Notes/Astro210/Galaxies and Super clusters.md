### Dark matter in galaxy clusters

- Use viral theorem...
- $$M ≈ 7.5 \frac{\sigma^2 r_h}{G}$$
- Where $\sigma$ is a velocity distribution, and $r_h$ is radius of the cluster
- If we look at the mass to light radio of the Coma Cluster, which is 250 (in solar units), we find a very different distribution as compared to our sun ($=1$), and the Milky Way ($∼ 10$)

### Galaxy collisions

- Can estimate velocity is cluster from velocity dispersion ($\sigma$) of galaxy motions
- $$v∼\sqrt{3}\sigma$$
- Can then compute collision rates
- In rich clusters about a $~ 50\%$ chance

#### Rates of collisions

Consider a galaxy of radii $R$ moving at velocity in, in an elapsed time $t$ it will collide with any other galaxy within a cylindrical volume $V(t)$ of radius $R^2$ and length $vt $
$$V(t) (4\pi^2) (vt)$$
If galaxy density is n then the total collisions is $N_\star$ and the average time to collision is $t_\star$
$$N_\star = nV(t) = v(4\pi R^2) (vt)$$
$$N_\star = 1 \rightarrow t_\star = \frac{1}{nv(4\pi R)}$$

#### Galaxies collide, but stars to not

If you repeat the previous equation but for stars, you find a $1$ in $10^8$ chance of stellar collision, in the age of the entire universe 

#### Physics of collisions

- Raise tidal bulges and resulting tidal tails
- They are inelastic, the kinetic energy is converted into funny motions of the stars themselves
